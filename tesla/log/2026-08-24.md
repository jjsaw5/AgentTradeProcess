### Why 356.65

The 10:30 bounce leg ran 354.16 → 358.70 and has spent ~45 minutes building a
shelf. The 5-min lows since 10:35 are **356.66 · 356.75 · 356.93 · 357.14 ·
357.15 · 357.42 · 357.77** — a rising floor. **356.65 is one cent under the
lowest of them.** A close below it breaks the higher-low structure that is the
entire reason to be long, and it sits below both flips (oi 357.94, vol 358.15),
so losing it puts spot decisively in negative-gamma territory.

Rejected alternatives:

- **357.94 / 358.15 (the flips)** — spot is oscillating *within* $0.21 of both.
  A stop there is inside the noise and would fire on nothing.
- **355.00** — the max-pain and put-wall confluence, $3.12 away. By the time it
  hit, a 0DTE call struck at 357.5 would be near worthless; it invalidates the
  thesis far too late to be a stop.
- **354.16 (bounce-leg low)** — same problem, worse.

### The stop buffer, and a spec conflict this exposes

`DATA_LAYER-TSLA.md` §1d says a **3-tick** buffer. This contract marks 1.95, so
its tick is **$0.01** and 3 ticks is **$0.03**.

**$0.03 is too tight here and the spec is wrong on this case.** The playbook's
original rule was a *15-cent* buffer, adopted because "fast tape gaps through
tight buffers — triggered but unfilled is the failure mode that matters." §1d
observed that 15c happens to equal 3 ticks *above* $3.00 and generalised to "3
ticks", which silently shrinks the tolerance by 5× on a sub-$3 contract.

This card uses **$0.10** — wide enough to fill on a fast 0DTE, tight enough not
to give away 15c on a $1.95 contract. Flagged for `/tsla-close` to resolve
properly; the spec is not amended mid-session.
