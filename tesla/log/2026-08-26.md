### 3a. The monitor's stop advice was wrong, and by how much

At 13:37 `/tsla-watch` reported the 6.35 trigger as too tight to survive noise
and pointed at 5.68. Measured noise supported that: adverse excursion below the
prior 5-min close, 12:00 onward, was median **$0.27**, p90 **$0.64**, and the
largest pullback inside the live up-leg was **$0.77** — against **$0.21** of
room. The 1-minute series gave median $0.09, p95 $0.49.

**The move that followed was not noise.** TSLA went 346.91 → **344.47** in 25
minutes.

| Stop | Exit | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| As placed (6.40 trigger, filled 6.25) | 6.25 | **−$40** |
| As advised (5.68) | ≈5.45 after slippage | ≈**−$120** |

The tighter stop saved roughly **$80**. Recorded per §9: the advice is not
revised on this outcome. A tight stop is a bet that every adverse move is real;
the measured distribution says that bet loses more often than it wins, and one
instance going the other way is not evidence against a distribution. **Graded on
execution, the stop was placed and honoured. Graded on P&L it beat the
alternative. Those are separate facts and both are recorded.**
