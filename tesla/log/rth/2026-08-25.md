### What today cannot test, and why it matters to the stopping rule

TSLA options expire **Monday, Wednesday, Friday**. On Tuesdays and Thursdays the
shortest DTE is 1 and there is no 0DTE contract in existence.

**Two of the six pre-registered predictions are written specifically for 0DTE:**
P1 names "0.30–0.60 delta TSLA 0DTE contracts," and P6 names "an ATM TSLA 0DTE
contract." Neither can be sampled today, and neither can be sampled on any
Thursday.

The schedule runs weekdays (`* * 1-5`), so **roughly two sessions in five produce
no evidence for the two headline predictions.** The stopping rule asks for "five
clean trading sessions"; if that is read as five *calendar* sessions, P1 and P6
will have three sessions of data at the stopping point, not five. If it is read
as five sessions in which each prediction is testable, the probe needs to run
about eight calendar sessions to close them out.

This is a scoping observation about the design, recorded per §9. **Nothing is
revised here** — not the predictions, not the schedule, not the stopping rule.
It sits alongside yesterday's finding that P1 and P5 declare windows that exclude
the exit sample, and that P2 asks a question point sampling cannot answer.

Today's usable evidence is P2 (morning-clear half), P3, and the P5 baseline.
Everything else is context.
