### P6 — a structural problem with the measurement, recorded before Sample C

The contract designated at Sample A for the A→C decay computation is the
**2026-08-24 352.50 CALL**, marked 2.855 at 09:50. At 13:35 it marks **3.125,
up 9.5%**, with delta 0.8262 and **$2.845 of intrinsic value**.

P6 predicts a >50% mark loss on a session finishing within ±0.5% of the 09:47
spot — a band of **351.96 to 355.50**. Spot is currently 355.345, near the top
of that band. If it finishes anywhere in the upper half, the contract's
intrinsic value alone floors its mark: at 355.50 intrinsic is $3.00, which is
**+5% against the 2.855 baseline**, not −50%. A >50% loss requires spot at
roughly 353.93 or below — only the bottom 56% of the qualifying band.

So on a day like today P6 can be *satisfied* only if spot drifts down inside the
band, and is otherwise not falsified so much as **unreachable**: "the ATM
contract at 09:47" stops being ATM the moment spot moves, and a call that goes
ITM cannot decay the way the prediction describes. This is a defect in how P6
selects its contract, visible before the result is known and recorded here for
that reason. **P6 is not revised** (§9). Sample C will compute and report the
actual A→C number regardless, plus the underlying's move, and state which case
the day fell into.
