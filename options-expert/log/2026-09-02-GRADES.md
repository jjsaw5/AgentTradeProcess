## Ticket 1 — XLE shares (written 2026-08-31) → **graded on the signal, not the fill**

**Ticket:** buy 2 XLE shares on a retest into 63.00–63.51 after 10:00 ET;
stop 62.70; R = $0.55/share; +1R → stop to breakeven; +2R → sell 1 share.

**What the market did:**

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Entry zone occupied (8/31, after 10:00) | **158 minutes**, low **63.15** at 11:24 ET |
| XLE at 2026-09-02 15:23 ET | **65.31** (+1.28% on the day, session low 64.31) |
| Move from the 63.15 zone low | **+$2.16/share ≈ +3.9R** |
| Position taken | **None. The owner saw the signal and declined it.** |
| Realized P&L | **$0** |

**Grade: the ticket A, the execution `NA_no_data`.**

The ticket's job was to name a level, a stop and a trigger before the fact. It
did, the trigger printed, and the level held — XLE has not traded back into the
zone since and is now roughly 4R above it. As a piece of pre-registration this
is the cleanest thing in the log so far.

There is no execution to grade because no position existed. That is
`NA_no_data`, not a failing grade: declining a signal is a legitimate decision
and the playbook does not require taking every trigger.

**What is a defect, and it is recorded as one:** *no reason was written down.*
§9 requires the expectation before the outcome; a refusal is a decision with an
expectation behind it, and this one is now unrecoverable. Two days later the
only honest statement available is "the signal was correct and we do not know
why it was passed." Reconstructing a reason now would be inventing one.

**Rule this suggests (NOT ratified, do not treat as binding):** when a written
trigger prints and is not taken, write one line in the ticket saying why, at the
time. A refusal costs nothing and teaches nothing unless it is logged.

**Counterfactual, stated as a bound and not as a result:** 2 shares × +$2.16 ≈
**+$4.32**. Real, but immaterial next to the +$105/−$62 swings the same account
took in options that week. The lesson here is about process fidelity, not money.
